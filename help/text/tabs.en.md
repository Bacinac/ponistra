# Tabs

A document divides into tabs and subtabs, each with content of its own, and under the open tab its headings are listed.

## How to use it

### A new tab

1. In the document's left column press {t:tab.new}.
2. For a subtab, open a tab's menu with a right click or a long press and choose {t:tab.newChild}.
3. You rename a tab in the same menu with {t:tree.rename}.

### Arranging tabs

1. Drag a tab by the handle beside its name to a new place in the list.
2. In the tab's menu choose {t:tab.up} or {t:tab.down}.
3. {t:tab.indent} turns the tab into a subtab of the one above it, and {t:tab.outdent} brings it back a level up.

### Headings

1. Under the open tab its {t:tab.headings} are listed, with numbers when the headings are numbered.
2. Press a heading to jump to its place in the text.

### Deleting

1. In the tab's menu choose {t:tab.delete}.
2. Confirm in the {t:tab.deleteTitle} dialog.

## How it works

Each tab has content of its own, and tables, presentations and text can stand in different tabs of the same document. A tab without a name shows as {t:tab.untitled}. The address in the browser leads to the open tab, so a link opens that very tab.

A tab is deleted together with its subtabs. The document is saved as a version before the deletion, so a deleted tab can be brought back [from a version](help:documents/versions).

## Limits

- Adding, moving, renaming and deleting tabs need the right to edit.
- A tab cannot be indented when there is no tab of the same level above it.
